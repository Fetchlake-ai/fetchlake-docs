Most investing claims are tested on the same data that suggested them, which makes them look better than they are. Insights does it the other way round.

## The method

<Steps>
  <Step title="Write the rules down first">
    Before any results are seen, each study fixes the trading rule, the shares it applies to, the time periods and the pass mark, and commits them with a date. The rules post is published first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Develop on one period">
    The idea is tried on a development period (for example 2005–2016). This is where it's allowed to be explored.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test once on years it hasn't seen">
    A final check runs **once**, on a later period kept aside from the start (a "holdout"). No adjusting afterwards.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Count the costs">
    Brokerage and the gap between buying and selling prices are subtracted, so the result is what a private investor could actually have earned.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish either way">
    The result goes out whether the idea passed or failed. The original rules post is kept, unchanged, above the results.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The data

- Every ASX-listed common stock, **including companies that later delisted**, so results aren't flattered by only looking at survivors.
- Shares need enough daily trading (a liquidity floor) so the results could be traded in practice.
- Returns are compared with the market as a whole.

## Reading a study

| Part | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Category** | The type of idea: momentum, fundamentals, reversal and risk, dividends and income. |
| **Verdict** | **Rejected** means it failed its pre-set pass mark after costs. Screens and how-to posts have no verdict, because they aren't tests. |
| **Rules and dates** | When the rules were fixed, and which years were used for development and the final test. |
| **Results tables** | Returns after costs compared with the market, for each test. |
| **What this is not** | The limits of the study. Read this part. |

## Things to keep in mind

- "No edge found" means the rule didn't beat the market after costs in the years tested. It doesn't prove the idea can never work.
- A study tests the exact rule written down. A different holding period or filter is a different idea.
- Past results, even honest ones, don't guarantee future returns.

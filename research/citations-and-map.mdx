Pick a collection in the [Library](/research/library) and use the view switch at the top of the list.

## Citations view

Fetchlake collects each paper's reference list from [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org) in the background, so the numbers grow as more papers are processed.

| Tab | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Most cited** | Works your collection cites most often, with how many of your papers cite each one (**Cited here**) and its worldwide citation count (**Cited worldwide**). |
| **Most cited outside your library** | Works your papers keep citing that you *don't* have yet. A ready-made reading list. |
| **Most cited authors** | The authors your collection relies on most. |

Click a work to see which of your papers cite it.

<Tip>
**Most cited outside your library** is the quickest way to find key papers you've missed. If something there is cited by half your collection, you probably need it.
</Tip>

## Map view

A picture of your collection, one dot per paper.

- **Timeline** spreads papers out by year, so you can see how the topic developed.
- **Network** pulls papers that cite each other closer together, so clusters show up as separate groups of work.
- **Shared-reference links** also connect papers that cite the same sources, even if they don't cite each other.
- **Hide unconnected papers** removes papers with no links, to make clusters easier to see.

Click a dot to open that paper's details.

## Tips

- The map is most useful once most of a collection has finished processing.
- A paper sitting on its own in the network may be off-topic, or may be the one that links two literatures. Either way, take a look at it.

The Library (**Dashboard → Library**) works like a reference manager. On the left is the list of collections, in the middle the list of items, and on the right the details of whichever item you've selected.

## Layout

| Area | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Sidebar** | **My Library** shows everything; click a collection to show only its items. |
| **Item list** | One row per paper. Click a row to see its details on the right. Double-click to open the reader. |
| **Item pane** | The paper's details, its extracted study record and its citations. |
| **View switch** | Switch between **Items**, **Citations** and **Map**. See [Citations and Map](/research/citations-and-map). |

## Adding papers

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a collection">
    Choose the **Collection for new files**. Every file has to go into a collection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the files">
    Drag PDFs anywhere onto the list, or use **Add files**. Each upload shows its progress.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for processing">
    Each item's status updates on its own. You can keep working while files process.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can also bring papers in from [Zotero or Google Drive](/research/connectors).

### Item statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Processing** | The PDF is being read and indexed. |
| **Extracting** | The AI is filling in the study record. |
| **Needs review** | Extraction finished. Check it against the paper. |
| **Ready** | Processed and reviewed. |
| **Reference only** | A reference with no file attached (for example, from an RIS import). |
| **Not extracted** | Indexed, but the study record hasn't been filled in. |
| **Failed** / **Extraction failed** | Something went wrong. Try the file again, or check it isn't a scanned image with no text. |

## Finding papers

- **Search** matches title, author, year and DOI.
- **Filter** by article type (research article, review, theoretical, policy, grey literature) and by status.

## The item pane

### Details
Title, authors, publication, year, volume, issue, pages, DOI, keywords and abstract. Edit anything that's wrong. Good details give you better citations and exports.

### Study record (extraction)
Fetchlake reads the paper and fills in a structured record. The fields depend on the article type. For a research article they include:

- Study design, methods, setting, country, population and sample size
- Intervention or exposure, and comparison
- **Outcomes**: each with its measure, timepoint, result, effect size and significance
- Main findings, limitations and a summary

**Re-extract** runs the AI again. It uses credits, so correct small mistakes by hand instead.

<Warning>
Extraction is a starting point, not a final answer. Always check effect sizes, sample sizes and significance against the paper before you rely on them.
</Warning>

## The reader

Double-click an item (or use **Read the document with its details and notes**) to open the reader: the item's details on the left, the PDF in the centre and your notes underneath. Notes save automatically.

## Tips

- Fill in **article type** first. It decides which extraction fields you see.
- Sort the list by status and work through **Needs review** items in one go.
- If a PDF won't read, it's usually a scan. Run it through OCR and upload it again.
